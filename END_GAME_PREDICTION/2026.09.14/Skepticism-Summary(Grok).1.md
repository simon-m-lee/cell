## What every document shares

None of the eight thinks Mitosis is a Riverpod/Bloc killer. All read the three packages as one causal graph (Cell = atom, Flow = timing, Tissue = governed books), and all treat the biological names as a composition plan rather than decoration. All cite the author’s own caveat: a counter does not need this machine. The disagreement is not “what was built.” It is “what happens to it.”

## The skeptical cluster (Kimi, Claude, GLM)

These three stay closest to public facts: one author, RC status, Dart’s center of gravity in Flutter UI, Phase 3 items that live on servers Dart barely occupies. Kimi is the sharpest. It gives explicit probabilities (≈60% maintenance-mode reference architecture, ≈25% agent-governance pivot, ≈10% niche shop, ≈5% archive) and reads dual licensing, caveats, and zero stars as *assessment hygiene* — a calling card more than a product. Claude adds the execution bottleneck: distributed cells, formal audit, and IDE support are years of work for a solo maintainer. GLM is the cleanest scenario table: most admired and borrowed from; tooling (causal replay) and agent timing decide whether it is more than that.

## The credulous cluster (Qwen, Dola, Gemini)

Qwen inflates the present (`opencell.dev`, `cell_organ` as if already in the published stack) and the future (TypeScript/Rust/Swift ports, architectural standardization on the order of Redux/Rx). Progressive disclosure is treated as a solved adoption strategy. Dola is better grounded in the demos (card auth, grid, dispatch) but then leaps to consulting, certified distributions, and aerospace/defense without evidence of a buyer. Gemini is the outlier of *kind*: it barely uses the published roadmap. Flow becomes DI, Tissue becomes a UI module, Mitosis becomes CLI scaffolding, and the endgame is a micro-frontend organism. That is a coherent essay about a different framework.

## The middle (DeepSeek, OpenAI)

DeepSeek restates the author’s phases faithfully and ends on an open bet: the use case is not typical *yet*. OpenAI is the most precise reading of the *design* (causal fabric, governance as runtime semantics, surface-vs-depth tension) and the least interested in GitHub sociology. Its skepticism is architectural (“capabilities may outrun the mental model”), not market-statistical.

## One-page verdict

Rank the eight as a spectrum from **portfolio artifact** (Kimi/Claude) to **inevitable standard** (Qwen). The documents that will age best are the ones that separate three claims the repo actually supports — (1) this is not UI state management, (2) the extra machinery is for audit/authority/replay, (3) more packages will keep arriving — from three claims it does not — (4) a market already exists in Dart, (5) progressive disclosure cancels cognitive load, (6) language ports or micro-frontends are implied. On that split, Kimi, Claude, and GLM are the skeptical records; Qwen and Gemini are the least reliable as forecasts, for opposite reasons: one over-believes the brochure, the other reviewed a brochure it invented.
