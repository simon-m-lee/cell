## The one-sentence answer

The comprehension document is **the record of the human checks where someone demonstrates they understand what they are about to approve** — chiefly the prediction check (gate C1) before the WalkThrough is accepted, plus the **decision log** (gate C3) of choices actually made. It exists so that "approved" means *understood and chosen*, not *scrolled past*.

## What it's for, concretely

1. **It makes approval mean comprehension.** Mitose 2.0 added it because v1.0 "could not tell whether a person understood what they approved." Before you accept a WalkThrough, gate C1 offers you the chance to **predict the outcome of three scenarios** (an ordinary path, a boundary/second interaction, and an interaction) *before* reading the expected results. Your predictions are recorded; then they're compared with what the BRD and coverage matrix say. A mismatch is treated as **a signal about the document first** — if you can't predict it, the document may be unclear — not as a failure of you.

2. **It is deliberately not a grade.** The rules are explicit: *"Describe, never grade. Record what a person predicted and what the BRD says. Do not rate the person and do not produce a comprehension score."* So it records, it doesn't score.

3. **It records the decision log (gate C3).** The same document holds the log of choices that were actually raised: layer placements where more than one layer could answer, resolved findings, accepted residual items, adopted or declined Recommendations, departures from a layer HowTo, and approved protected changes. It's shown before ARCHITECTURE is written, so the design's *why* is traceable by decision ID rather than restated as prose.

4. **It's the provenance of human judgment.** Just as the ledger records where *evidence* came from, the comprehension document records where *decisions and understanding* came from — and, crucially, **who** made them. The agent never invents an approval; if a decision wasn't actually raised and answered, it doesn't appear as if it was.

5. **Skipping is allowed and recorded.** C1 can be taken, skipped, or declined — and that outcome is written down. Declining isn't a failure; pretending it happened would be.

## What it is *not*

- **Not a quiz or a certification** — it doesn't test the person's skill.
- **Not the ledger** — it holds no requirement results; the ledger holds those.
- **Not a place to record approvals the agent didn't receive** — approvals belong to named people.
- **Not mandatory to "pass"** — taking C1 is offered once; declining is a valid, recorded choice.
